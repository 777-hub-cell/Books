## Binding supplied canon

- A1, A2 and A3 share a fairytale universe.
- The dragon raises an abandoned human girl as his daughter. Humans falsely accuse and kill her. His retaliatory violence leads to periodic human sacrifices that he actually kills.
- A new innocent woman is attacked, offered, survives a raid that wounds his eye, helps him recover, and develops a consequential relationship with him.
- Three main endings: one sad, two good. Only the designated good ending supplies the shared timeline.
- In that canonical future, the dragon and the heroine have descendants. Their descendants inherit dragon qualities, conquer territory over generations, establish a powerful kingdom and rule through considerable prosperity.
- A3’s prince and its unknowingly illegitimate chosen girl belong to that bloodline. Their exact relationship and the artifact’s purpose remain with A3; A2 does not decide them.

## Relative chronology

1. Ordan discovers Linnet as a baby; no biological parentage is invented.
2. At eleven: goat and bread scene. At nineteen: school hinge, flood measurements, arrest and murder.
3. Dragon attacks river towns and an army. Surviving towns negotiate a revocable, nonmagical charter.
4. Twenty-seven offerings over the following 108 years. Edda is sent as the twenty-eighth at the 112-year mark; she is not killed.
5. Raid on Edda’s ninth day; departure on day twenty-three; reunion and independent residence; weeks of investigation, spring bridge work, chosen romance or friendship. No romance is demanded during captivity or wound care.
6. Canonical hearings span the summer; marriage before winter; Teren’s birth four years after hearings.
7. Unnumbered later generations: inherited power, conquest, kingdom, prosperity. Leave exact genealogy, reigns, dates, legitimacy branches, dynasty and kingdom names to A3’s continuity owner.

## Local magical rules and limits

- A complete form change takes nine heartbeats. Intermediate living tissue is unarmoured; fire breath is unavailable because heat maintains the change. The attackers expect a post-feeding weakness and accidentally strike during a true vulnerability they did not anticipate.
- Human and dragon forms are living bodies, not illusions. Human form can produce children. Neither form restores a destroyed eye. The wound scars the heat channels permanently.
- Summerstones store transferred dragon heat and release it. They cannot contain a soul, resurrect a person or independently heal injuries. Small stones warm rooms; great stones require iron containment and controlled discharge. Excess heat can be absorbed by Ordan at mortal risk because of his injury.
- Edda supplies ordinary nursing, clean cloth and water, warmth, rest and a steady rhythm during a controlled change. Ordan’s own biology performs the repair. Edda gains no unexplained healing power.
- Descendants inherit heat tolerance, sensitivity to stored dragon heat, capacity to retain and direct heat, and endurance, with variable expression. They remain vulnerable to illness and injury. No universal full transformation, immortality, invulnerability or guarantee of moral fitness is assigned to all descendants.
- The sacrifice bargain is voluntary and political. No enchantment, feeding requirement or secret innocence removes Ordan’s responsibility.

## Ending boundaries

**A House with Two Doors — canonical good.** Public hearings, reparations, a chosen romantic partnership, marriage, Teren and the later bloodline. The initial household does not itself conquer. Later conquest is not excused by the parents’ virtues.

**The Open Mountain — alternate good.** Refuge and cooperative settlement; romance or friendship according to choice. In the romantic variant the pair choose a life without children. There is no dynasty in this timeline. Voss may escape immediate arrest; testimony and pensions remain ongoing political work. This ending is not a failed canonical attempt.

**The Last Crossing — alternate sad.** Ordan dies containing the heat while families escape. Voss survives or dies according to the rescue decision. No bloodline is established. Edda survives and continues her own life. His death does not balance or erase his earlier killings.

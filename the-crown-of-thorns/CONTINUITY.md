# Continuity record

## Status and authority

The supplied A1 brief is authoritative. The A2 and A3 briefs were retrieved and read on 14 November 2026 to check conflicts. Books was empty when first inspected; no existing shared-world bible was available there. The Category B document found among project materials is not applied as an A1 hero/villain mandate.

**Approved facts:** three independent Category A books share a world; A1 has a memory-cursed, self-sustaining town enclosed for roughly two centuries; attempting to leave kills after three steps; the protagonist is an initially unidentified princess; narration is third-person limited; major choices persist.

**Author-developed local facts:** every named character, Fenmere and the Fen Basin, the 197-year interval, Maud's local crown, the siege, the pledge instrument, and the four ending designs. They implement creative authority delegated in A1. They do not become retroactive user-approved shared-world facts simply by appearing here. No A1 ending has been selected as shared-universe canon.

**Reserved:** the basin's continental position; current outside kingdoms; who conducted the siege; the fate of Maud after she left the chamber; the exact calendar relation to A2/A3; any relationship to their royal dynasty. No dragon ancestry is assigned to Reed or Maud. The crown here is not the selecting artifact in A3.

## Geography and society

Fenmere is the main town in a broad hill basin, with outlying farms, grazing, orchards, forest, a river, salt spring, flax cultivation, bog iron and charcoal production. The full circuit takes longer than a casual town walk. The ward follows the basin rim and also crosses waterways. Population is deliberately unspecified. Material constraints remain after the ward ends: roads, trade and infrastructure must be repaired. The outside exists, but its current political geography is not narrated without discovery.

Food comes from mixed crops, animals and river resources. Crafts use paired instruction and written/pictorial checks. Household charts preserve facts, not felt affection. Market measures are independently checked. Government has a steward, a clerk, public craft representatives and household witnesses; none is assumed a modern democratic state. Oren's position is local. Reed's old title does not settle the new constitution.

## Local magical rules

1. The instrument encloses those within the refuge and their descendants; the guarantor remains in suspended sleep and does not physically age through the interval. Her initial personal history is the first deposit, explaining her empty autobiographical memory on waking. It is not presented as a world-wide time system. Rooted approach signs divert outside roads away from the concealed refuge and inside roads towards it.
2. Outbound departure kills on the third step. The river accounts establish that carrying, carts and boats do not avoid it: the ward counts successive outbound advances. Animals and loose matter are not bound. No living person is used for a new test during the investigation.
3. The monthly bell takes an unheld autobiographical connection-memory, generally working from distant connections towards household and identity. Practical skills, literacy and externally written facts survive. Holding one memory present through speech exposes others; it does not let a resident nominate a painless payment. When Reed rehearses clues, facts remain but recognition of Pip is lost. When she rehearses Pip, reconstruction from records does not restore the lost experience.
4. The Keeper stores the memories of living owners in pledge; death closes an owner's account. Returning memories does not revive anyone. Sera's own living memories may include Lenn; Lenn's own stored memories cannot be transferred back after death.
5. Royal status permits a guarantor's declaration for this pledge; it grants no generic magical ability or claim to other kingdoms.
6. Release accounting requires Crown, Witness and original roll. Human suppression of the roll and terms prevents accountability. The Keeper has no independent policy for checking whether the siege outside is over. Its literalism does not excuse Oren's actions.
7. Divided return needs three independent willing household groups to call, hold and verify the return, plus present anchors. Reed carries unclaimed shares and retains uncertain fragments. Nobody can manufacture consent through a royal order.
8. A lone return requires a recovered conscious connection to the pledge; it consumes Reed's autobiographical identity while returning other living owners' memories. Her body and procedural skills survive.
9. Destruction opens the ward but permanently destroys the pledged relationship-memories and their still-attached recognition. Retained factual records and skills allow rebuilding; affection cannot be compelled by them.
10. Renewal restores the sleep and doubles the tithe until the cut root repairs. It is a complete tragic outcome, with surviving release instructions, not a promise that future rescue certainly succeeds.

## Cross-book checks

A2's canonical good ending creates the dragon bloodline. A3's prince and selected girl descend from that line. A1 establishes neither a new origin for that bloodline nor a competing selecting artifact. No cameo, named kingdom or absolute chronology is imposed. Future authors should resolve continental placement and dates jointly before publishing a common map.

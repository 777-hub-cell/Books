# Writing research — The Crown of Thorns

Access date for all successful sources: 14 November 2026. Research began before drafting and continued during revision. Notes are paraphrases, not quotations. This is a craft log, not evidence that any manuscript has passed an independent editorial assessment.

## Before drafting

1. Writing Excuses, **6.26: Mystery Plotting**. https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-6-26-mystery-plotting/
   Work backwards from a solution; separate encountering evidence, interpreting it, and recognising its implications. Applied to Reed's three scars, the damaged signature, the submerged release clause, and the chamber portrait. The crown's role is inferable before the Keeper explains it. The identity revelation occurs in chapter eight, in Reed's presence. A clue ledger is in CONTINUITY.md.

2. Writing Excuses, **9.23: World Building Without Breaking Viewpoint**. https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-9-23-world-building-without-breaking-viewpoint/
   Reveal a society through what a viewpoint character encounters while trying to do something. Applied to carrying repaired sacks, checking flour, market measures, the well list and household charts. Reed's ignorance permits questions without making local people recite their whole history. Food, salt, fuel, iron, education and caregiving appear as work.

3. inkle, **Writing with ink**, official documentation. https://github.com/inkle/ink/blob/master/Documentation/WritingWithInk.md
   Branches may gather while state preserves consequences. Applied to separate rescue/death, notebook/gift, public/private, memory, bridge, hearing/seizure and final-investigation scenes. Later beats and ending access depend on saved flags. The implementation uses NIGHT's engine, not ink; this source informed structure only.

## During drafting and revision

4. Writing Excuses, **5.4: Creating Suspense**. https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-5-4-creating-suspense/
   Distinguish curiosity about a hidden explanation from anticipation of a known danger. The reader knows the third-step rule early, witnesses its cost or a narrow rescue, and later anticipates the bell. The mystery remains why the refuge became permanent. The announced renewal deadline supplies pressure without a real-time browser timer.

5. Lindy Ryan, **What Isn't Said Still Screams: Writing Subtext in Horror Fiction**, Jane Friedman. https://janefriedman.com/what-isnt-said-still-screams-writing-subtext-in-horror-fiction/
   Physical actions and evasions can carry a scene's emotional work. Applied to Sera's fourth bowl, Oren covering a portrait, Pip moving his chair, and the boat rib scene. Dialogue has practical immediate purposes. Characters are not required to deliver a thematic speech before helping someone.

6. Writing Excuses, **5.17: Dialog Exercises**. https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-5-17-dialog-exercises/
   Distinguish speakers through diction, social role and sentence patterns. Pip asks concrete, inconvenient questions; Sera organises care; Oren qualifies and controls information; Marta checks records and acknowledges uncertainty. The Keeper responds narrowly because it is an inflexible local instrument. Revision removed several places where its explanation would otherwise make the human cast passive.

7. Brandon Sanderson, **Sanderson's Second Law**. https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-second-law
   Constraints and costs make magical solutions legible. Applied to the prohibition on reviving the dead, the distinction between written facts and emotional recognition, the original roll requirement, and the explicit costs of all four resolutions. These are local rules for this bargain, not universal rules imposed on the other books. First encountered after the initial draft, then used to audit the cure and ending gates.

8. Sheila Williams, **ChatGPT and Me**, Asimov's Science Fiction, January/February 2024. https://asimovs.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Editorial_CHAT-GPT-and-Me_JanFeb24.pdf
   A primary editorial account of prohibited AI submissions and their impact on submissions work. This is an editor's judgement, not a universal detector or proof about any particular sentence. Applied as a reason to review actual scenes for individual motives, specific work, causal action and consequences, and to avoid claiming that fluency alone equals editorial quality. This project is openly AI-assisted and is not represented as a human-only magazine submission.

## Criticisms investigated and concrete revision checks

The assignment identifies repetitive cadence, generic imagery, interchangeable voices, artificial dialogue, exposition, unearned twists and emotion explained twice as concerns. These are editorial checks, not claims that a source scientifically establishes a list of AI markers. Sources 1, 2, 5 and 6 supply craft alternatives; source 8 supplies first-hand publishing context. News reports found in the search were not used as technical authority. Neil Clarke's blog could not be retrieved directly and is not treated as a successfully read source.

- Repetitive drama: quiet labour scenes interrupt danger; endings include meals, visitor hours, tools and practical rebuilding.
- Generic worldbuilding: isolation has an economy, inherited repair records, apprentices, independent tallies and consent problems.
- Interchangeable voices: the mill scene and boat scene have different aims from the council and the accounting scenes.
- Convenient amnesia: the bell follows a stated schedule, rehearsed memory remains present, and lost recognition remains lost after a branch rejoins. No scene invents a miraculous cure through a written note.
- Convenient finale: release terms appear under the bridge and in Marta's copied ledger before the full accounting and final selection. The lone-vessel gate requires a deliberately recovered connection; the shared gate requires enough willing groups.
- Shallow alternative endings: each outcome has a climax and a distinct aftermath. The return route includes lasting uncertainty; sacrifice does not make Reed a trophy; destruction does not demand gratitude; renewal records the ongoing harm.

## Limits

The curse is invented magic. It is not a model of dementia, traumatic amnesia or any clinical condition. No medical claims are based on it. There has been no external sensitivity read, independent developmental edit or reader panel. Automated checks verify authored state and passages, not literary merit.
